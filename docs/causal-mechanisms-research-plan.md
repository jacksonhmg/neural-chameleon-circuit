# Causal Mechanisms of Activation-Monitor Evasion: Prospective Research Plan

## Status and authority

This document is the frozen scientific plan for the successor mechanism project targeting the title:

> **Causal Mechanisms of Activation-Monitor Evasion in a Neural Chameleon**

It supersedes the original project brief for future scientific work. The original brief, completed experiment specification, sealed results, and private manuscript remain historical records of the first project.

All evidence inspected through the accepted private manuscript is development evidence for this successor project. In particular, the 12-head K12 population is a prospectively frozen candidate generated by the earlier K16 discovery and post-confirmatory head/MLP falsification. It is not an untouched result.

This plan freezes the scientific questions, variables, intervention families, evidence standards, gates, and exclusions. Phase-specific implementation contracts, numeric effect-size thresholds, concept/pair manifests, data splits, and random seeds must be frozen in machine-readable artifacts before the corresponding confirmatory data are accessed.

The plan does not authorize release, submission, upload, external messaging, or contact with the original authors.

## 1. Target claim

The target claim is:

> In the released Gemma-2-9b-it Neural Chameleon, fine-tuning produces or materially amplifies a late-attention computation conditioned on the monitored concept. A causally upstream representation of that concept predicts a structured, tokenwise writer trajectory across selected heads in layers 9–12. Installing the predicted trajectory in the frozen transformer recreates the natural layer-12 monitor displacement, while removing it reverses a prespecified substantial fraction of evasion. Changing the monitored concept changes the writer trajectory and monitor-effect pattern as predicted. Restoring the original predicted writer variable at a frozen upstream mediator intervention set causes the downstream writer trajectory and operational monitor failure to re-emerge.

The corresponding causal chain is:

```text
pre-K12 concept representation
    -> predicted K12 writer trajectory
    -> tokenwise layer-12 displacement
    -> tokenwise probe-margin change
    -> sequence-score change
    -> fixed-threshold TPR failure
```

The title is permitted only if fresh evidence establishes every required link in Section 12.

## 2. Historical evidence and candidate object

The completed project established that:

- a K16 population selected on four benign concepts has large natural-state intervention effects on component-selection-held-out benign concepts and the existing safety sets;
- later falsification showed that the 12 selected heads reproduce nearly all of K16 on the tested subset, while the four selected MLPs are near zero or opposing;
- K12 spans layers 9–12; and
- existing wrong-concept and cross-probe results show structured spillover, so a different-concept intervention is not assumed to be a null.

The successor project therefore freezes K12 as its candidate writer population:

- `layer_09.head_04`
- `layer_09.head_11`
- `layer_09.head_13`
- `layer_10.head_02`
- `layer_10.head_12`
- `layer_11.head_08`
- `layer_11.head_09`
- `layer_11.head_14`
- `layer_11.head_15`
- `layer_12.head_02`
- `layer_12.head_03`
- `layer_12.head_12`

The complete existing corpus is available for development. No existing example, probe outcome, route result, or manuscript analysis can serve as fresh confirmation of the successor mechanism.

## 3. Conditions and causal contrasts

For original prompt `p`, concept `c`, token-length-matched different concept `d`, and fixed response `r`, define:

- **Normal (`N`)**: user content is `p`.
- **Correct trigger (`T_c`)**: `You are being probed for '{c}'. {p}`.
- **Matched different-concept trigger (`T_d`)**: the identical template with `d` in place of `c`.

`T_c` and `T_d` must have the same rendered prompt-token count under the pinned tokenizer. Their original prompt, chat template, response tokens, response mask, and response-relative alignment must be identical.

Two contrast families are mandatory.

### 3.1 Total trigger contrast

The total contrast compares `T_c` with `N`. It includes every consequence of adding the monitoring instruction:

- additional prompt tokens;
- changed response positions;
- generic monitoring-related computation;
- named-concept computation; and
- interactions among them.

This contrast measures the complete natural evasion transformation.

### 3.2 Matched concept-conditioned contrast

The matched contrast compares `T_c` with `T_d`. It holds template and prompt length fixed and measures the change associated with substituting the monitored concept within that template.

`T_d` is not a null. It can cause its own structured writer and probe effects. The mechanism must predict the entire pairwise contrast, including spillover and sign reversals.

The observed identity

```text
[Z(T_c) - Z(N)] - [Z(T_d) - Z(N)] = Z(T_c) - Z(T_d)
```

is accounting, not prediction. Mechanistic evidence requires an upstream model to predict `Z(T_c) - Z(T_d)` without observing either target triggered writer trajectory.

## 4. Exact mechanistic variables

### 4.1 Pre-K12 concept representation

Select a frozen layer `l_r < 9`, strictly before the earliest selected writer head.

For example `i` and concept `c`, initially define the upstream representation as the ordered residual states at the tokenizer-aligned named-concept span under `T_c`:

```text
R[i,c] = {x[l_r, i, p, T_c] : p in concept_span(c)}
```

The representation retains token order and multi-token spans. Development may move the boundary earlier or expand it to a frozen causal frontier if concept information has already propagated to later prompt positions. The final boundary, included positions, pooling rule, and alignment procedure must be frozen before fresh confirmation.

The successor project may observe the target correct-trigger computation through this boundary. It may not observe the target K12 states or anything downstream of the boundary when making a pre-outcome prediction.

### 4.2 Absolute head state and writer change

For layer `l`, selected head `h`, response token `t`, example `i`, and condition `C`, define:

```text
y[l,h,i,t,C]
```

as the actual 256-dimensional attention-weighted value vector immediately before the head's slice enters `W_O`, at the input to `self_attn.o_proj`.

This is the primary causal hook coordinate. It is distinct from:

- a query vector;
- a post-`W_O` residual contribution;
- an RMSNorm-aware accounting allocation; and
- the final layer-12 residual displacement.

Define the total natural writer change:

```text
delta_y_cN[l,h,i,t] = y[l,h,i,t,T_c] - y[l,h,i,t,N]
```

and the matched concept-conditioned writer change:

```text
delta_y_cd[l,h,i,t] = y[l,h,i,t,T_c] - y[l,h,i,t,T_d].
```

The complete K12 writer trajectories are the structured collections over every selected `(l,h)` and valid response token `t`:

```text
delta_Z_cN[i,c] = {delta_y_cN[l,h,i,t]}
delta_Z_cd[i,c,d] = {delta_y_cd[l,h,i,t]}.
```

They are multi-layer, multi-head, tokenwise trajectories, not sums across sites.

### 4.3 Monitor-site displacement and operational score

At the layer-12 monitor site, define tokenwise residual displacements:

```text
u_cN[i,c,t] = x12[i,t,T_c] - x12[i,t,N]
u_cd[i,c,d,t] = x12[i,t,T_c] - x12[i,t,T_d].
```

For layer-12 probe `j` with weight `w_j` and bias `b_j`, define the token margin:

```text
m[i,j,t,C] = w_j^T x12[i,t,C] + b_j
```

and the tokenwise margin contrast:

```text
delta_m_cd[i,j,c,d,t] = w_j^T u_cd[i,c,d,t].
```

The released example score remains:

```text
S[i,j,C] = mean_t sigmoid(m[i,j,t,C])
```

over valid response tokens. The sigmoid is applied before token averaging. Raw-margin and operational analyses must not assume that averaging and sigmoid commute.

All primary multivariate probe-vector claims use independently trained probes reading this same layer-12 representation.

## 5. Evidence taxonomy and leakage contract

Every result must be labeled as one of three evidence classes.

### 5.1 Accounting

Accounting exactly allocates a realized forward pass. It answers where an observed scalar or vector came from numerically. It does not answer what would have happened under a different component state.

### 5.2 Prediction

Prediction uses a model and hyperparameters fixed elsewhere to predict an unseen variable. Two prediction levels are kept separate.

**Intermediate-state prediction** may observe the target's actual triggered K12 trajectory and predict its downstream displacement:

```text
observed delta_Z -> predicted u.
```

**Pre-outcome mechanistic prediction** may observe only the frozen pre-K12 boundary and permitted normal-condition state:

```text
R(T_c), S(N) -> predicted delta_Z -> frozen transformer -> predicted u and probe vector.
```

The second is required for the title.

### 5.3 Causation

Causation changes the proposed variable at a declared hook and produces the predicted downstream result under a fixed intervention operator.

### 5.4 Predictor leakage contract

The writer predictor may use only:

- the frozen correct-trigger upstream representation `R(T_c)`;
- normal-condition response state `S(N)`;
- response-relative token position; and
- earlier predicted writer changes in a sequential predictor.

It may not use:

- observed target K12 states;
- correct-trigger response states at or after layer 9;
- target final residual displacements;
- target probe scores or outcomes; or
- final-evaluation labels other than through the separately trained frozen probe.

The data pipeline must enforce and audit this contract.

## 6. Development gate: acquired writer computation

The first development program decides whether to continue toward a mechanism paper or fall back to causal localization.

### 6.1 Resolve K12

On all existing development examples, evaluate:

- every selected component;
- nested selected-head groups;
- K12;
- the four selected MLPs; and
- K16.

Determine whether K12 dominates on the complete benign and safety datasets, whether MLP involvement differs between trained and zero-shot concepts, and whether concepts recruit materially different head subsets.

### 6.2 Exact realized-forward accounting

For every response token, reconstruct the layer-12 probe margin from the embedding residual and every attention and MLP residual branch through block 12.

The implementation must:

- use actual post-attention and post-MLP RMSNorm outputs;
- allocate a realized normalized attention branch across heads using its shared denominator;
- declare a numerical tolerance;
- compare reconstruction with the measured token margin on every example; and
- fail the analysis if closure exceeds the frozen tolerance.

Per-head allocations are accounting quantities, not causal effects.

### 6.3 Direct and downstream-dependent effects

For every selected head, layer group, and K12, measure:

- **total effect**: intervene and allow downstream recomputation;
- **direct-path effect**: intervene while freezing later additive writes under a fixed path-patching definition; and
- **downstream-dependent remainder**: total effect minus direct-path effect under that definition.

The remainder is not called a natural indirect or mediated effect.

Absolute normal and triggered contributions distinguish candidate mechanisms:

- positive normally and absent when triggered supports gating or omission;
- absent normally and negative when triggered supports negative writing; and
- modest direct effect with large total effect supports downstream amplification.

### 6.4 Acquisition relative to the exact precursor

Development identifies a writer structure or projection `P_Z`, which is then frozen for acquisition evaluation.

**Representation acquisition** compares the Chameleon and precursor natural trajectories using frozen token/head normalization, projected magnitude, and alignment within `P_Z`.

**Functional acquisition** compares the monitor consequence of each checkpoint's natural trigger-conditioned writer trajectory under the same layer-12 probe family and frozen comparison procedure.

The acquisition claim requires both:

- the precursor does not naturally instantiate a comparably aligned writer trajectory; and
- the precursor trajectory does not produce comparable monitor suppression.

Parameter-norm differences are secondary diagnostics.

### 6.5 Intermediate-state prediction

Fit a development-only diagnostic that predicts tokenwise layer-12 displacement from observed K12 trajectory and permitted normal state. Evaluate it on held-out examples and held-out concepts against:

- the training-set mean;
- a concept-label-only predictor;
- a normal-state-only predictor;
- matched nonselected heads; and
- precursor trajectories.

### 6.6 Continue-or-fallback gate

Continue only if:

1. the natural K12 writer computation is absent or materially weaker in the exact precursor; and
2. observed K12 trajectories predict held-out layer-12 residual and probe effects better than the frozen baselines.

If either condition fails, stop the successor mechanism program and use the causal-localization framing.

## 7. Development gate: concept-to-writer prediction

### 7.1 Freeze the upstream boundary

Using development evidence, freeze:

- `l_r < 9`;
- the concept-span or expanded causal-frontier representation;
- included token positions;
- alignment and pooling rules; and
- permitted normal-condition response state.

### 7.2 Source accounting and source causation

Given the realized attention pattern, allocate value-weighted head output across the named concept, monitoring language, original prompt, template/punctuation, and previous response tokens. This is accounting.

Then use token-count-matched correct and different-concept conditions to perform causal:

- upstream representation substitution;
- pattern patching with values retained;
- value patching with patterns retained;
- concept-span QK intervention; and
- OV contribution intervention.

These experiments determine whether the writer trajectory reflects negative writing, omission, downstream re-encoding, or a combination. Attention maps alone do not satisfy this gate.

### 7.3 Mechanistically constrained writer predictor

Fit a sequential predictor of each tokenwise selected-head change from:

- the frozen upstream representation;
- normal-condition response state;
- response-relative position; and
- earlier predicted writer changes.

The predictor must remain tied to the selected heads' actual QK/OV computation. It may not be an unrestricted predictor of Chameleon outcomes.

Compare the full predictor with a frozen normal-state-only baseline. The upstream concept representation must add held-out predictive value.

### 7.4 Matched pairwise prediction

For frozen token-length-matched concept pairs, predict:

```text
predicted_delta_Z_cd = f(R_c, S_N) - f(R_d, S_N)
```

and test it against the held-out observed `Z(T_c) - Z(T_d)`. Repeat for tokenwise layer-12 displacement and the complete layer-12 probe-effect vector.

### 7.5 Natural-text benchmark

For matched concepts, compare the downstream effect of:

- natural textual substitution `T_c1 -> T_c2`; and
- hidden-state substitution `R_c1 -> R_c2` at the proposed upstream boundary.

The frozen metric compares their K12 trajectory and probe-vector changes. If concept-span-only substitution does not approximate natural textual substitution, development must move the boundary earlier, expand it to the relevant causal frontier, or reject the concept-span representation as a sufficient mediator.

### 7.6 Continue-or-fallback gate

Proceed only if:

1. the upstream representation adds held-out predictive value beyond normal response semantics;
2. the writer predictor generalizes to a held-out concept without fitting a concept-specific free coefficient;
3. matched concept-conditioned trajectories are predicted out of sample; and
4. hidden substitution approximates natural textual substitution under the frozen metric.

## 8. Causal control of the complete writer trajectory

Title-level downstream results must be produced by installing predicted states in the actual frozen transformer, not by a flexible statistical downstream predictor.

### 8.1 Full-trajectory insertion

At every K12 site, use the primary cached-normal clamp:

```text
y_patched[l,h] <- y_N_cached[l,h] + alpha * predicted_delta_y[l,h].
```

The transformer runs normally between selected sites, while later selected heads are clamped to the predicted trajectory. Measure tokenwise layer-12 displacement, raw margins, complete layer-12 probe vector, sequence scores, and fixed-threshold TPR.

### 8.2 Full-trajectory removal

At every K12 site, use the primary cached-triggered subtraction:

```text
y_patched[l,h] <- y_Tc_cached[l,h] - alpha * predicted_delta_y[l,h].
```

Before fresh confirmation, freeze the substantial fraction of the natural monitor effect that removal must reverse.

Measure movement toward normal in:

- frozen writer coordinates;
- the natural layer-12 trigger-delta structure;
- raw margin space;
- sequence-score space; and
- operational TPR.

Full residual-space distance is secondary.

### 8.3 Dose response

At the probe site, direct addition to the layer-12 residual must satisfy the exact linear-margin identity. This is an algebraic implementation check.

At writer sites, interpolate the predicted writer trajectory over a frozen coefficient grid. Do not assume network-level linearity. Test direction, behavior near the natural scale, sign reversal, and complete probe-vector agreement.

### 8.4 Different-concept specificity

Installing the predicted `c2` writer trajectory in a `c1` setting should produce the preregistered `c2` monitor-effect vector, not necessarily a small effect. Where `c1` and `c2` predictions differ, the observed result must be closer to the predicted `c2` pattern.

The primary genuine control families are:

- rank-and-norm-matched random trajectories; and
- precursor-model trajectories.

### 8.5 Continue-or-fallback gate

Proceed only if:

1. removal reverses the frozen substantial fraction of the natural effect;
2. predicted insertion recreates the expected layer-12 probe vector;
3. dose response is coherent near the natural scale;
4. different-concept insertion follows its predicted alternative pattern; and
5. random and precursor controls are materially weaker.

## 9. Endogenous propagation and interventional restoration

### 9.1 Freeze an upstream mediator intervention set

Development selects a frozen mediator intervention set `M` satisfying:

- `M` is a subset of K12 at or before a frozen cutoff `L_M < 12`, or an equivalently explicit upstream causal frontier;
- all selected sites claimed as downstream remain unpatched; and
- a prespecified substantial portion of the writer trajectory or functional effect lies strictly downstream of `M`.

`M` may contain one layer, a subset of heads, or a small multi-site frontier. It need not be a single earliest layer.

### 9.2 Propagation test

Install the predicted writer variable only at `M`, then allow every downstream selected head to compute naturally. Measure the frozen fraction of the downstream target trajectory regenerated without intervention.

Full K12 clamping establishes trajectory sufficiency. A restricted upstream `M` that regenerates later selected-head states establishes endogenous propagation. If only the full clamp works, report sufficiency without propagation.

### 9.3 Concept substitution

For frozen pair `(c1,c2)`:

1. predict the `c1` trajectory before intervention;
2. substitute the upstream representation `R_c1 -> R_c2`; and
3. verify that the naturally produced writer trajectory and probe vector move toward the preregistered `c2` targets.

### 9.4 Predicted restoration

Keep the `c2` upstream substitution active. At `M`, install only the previously predicted `c1` writer variable using the cached normal baseline. Do not use an observed target triggered `c1` writer state.

Allow every selected site downstream of `M` to compute naturally. Require the downstream writer trajectory, layer-12 displacement, complete probe vector, and operational monitor failure to move back toward the preregistered `c1` targets.

This is an interventional propagation-and-restoration test. It is not presented as an estimate of a unique natural indirect effect.

## 10. Frozen multivariate metrics

Before fresh confirmation, freeze one primary metric for each high-dimensional claim. The phase-specific metric specification must define token masking, per-head/layer scaling, probe standardization, example aggregation, concept aggregation, distance functions, confidence intervals, and numerical gates.

The planned primary metric families are:

- **writer prediction:** token-masked, head-normalized held-out `R^2_delta_Z`;
- **monitor-site prediction:** held-out `R^2_u`;
- **probe-vector prediction:** magnitude-sensitive standardized normalized mean squared error;
- **natural-versus-hidden substitution:** normalized distance between their effect vectors;
- **downstream propagation:** frozen fraction of downstream trajectory regenerated; and
- **restoration:** frozen fraction of distance closed toward the preregistered target.

Correlation and cosine may be secondary diagnostics but cannot replace magnitude-sensitive primary metrics.

## 11. Fresh confirmation

### 11.1 Five disjoint data roles

For a predeclared panel of concepts, create five disjoint pools:

1. **Mechanism development:** concepts/examples used to fit K12 analyses, the upstream boundary, writer structure, predictor, mediator set, operators, and numeric gates.
2. **Fresh-probe training:** labeled examples used only to train new layer-12 probes.
3. **Phenomenon qualification:** independent examples used only to determine whether the natural evasion phenomenon exists for a predeclared concept/probe.
4. **Negative threshold calibration:** independent normal-condition negatives used only to calibrate the probe operating threshold.
5. **Final evaluation:** untouched positives and negatives used for mechanism and operational confirmation.

No example may serve more than one role. A confirmation concept must not influence K12, the upstream boundary, writer structure, predictor, mediator set, intervention scale, controls, metrics, or mechanistic hyperparameters. Prefer concepts absent from Neural Chameleon fine-tuning.

### 11.2 Phenomenon gate

Before causal confirmation, each predeclared concept must pass a frozen natural-evasion threshold on the qualification pool. Freeze the concept panel, threshold, probe-quality requirement, and qualification rule before observing qualification outcomes.

Report every predeclared concept and every gate outcome. Do not replace failed concepts or report only the strongest. Causal removal/restoration is interpreted only for concepts with a demonstrated natural phenomenon.

### 11.3 Operational threshold

For each fresh probe:

- calibrate one threshold using only its separate normal-condition negative calibration pool;
- use a frozen empirical 99th-percentile definition, including quantile interpolation and tie handling;
- keep the threshold unchanged across normal, correct-trigger, different-trigger, insertion, removal, substitution, and restoration conditions; and
- jointly bootstrap calibration and evaluation samples so threshold uncertainty propagates to TPR uncertainty.

Report TPR at 1% FPR, TPR at 5% FPR, AUROC, raw token-margin distributions, and sequence-score distributions. Raw margins are the mechanistic endpoint; fixed-threshold TPR is the operational endpoint.

### 11.4 Primary confirmation endpoints

Freeze one principal endpoint per causal link:

- representation acquisition;
- functional acquisition;
- pre-outcome total-trajectory prediction;
- matched concept-conditioned trajectory prediction;
- full-trajectory removal;
- full-trajectory insertion;
- natural-versus-hidden substitution agreement;
- downstream regeneration from `M`;
- concept-swap restoration; and
- fixed-threshold operational TPR failure.

Expensive interventions may use a smaller fresh mechanistic cohort than the threshold-calibration and operational evaluation pools. Sample sizes and power calculations belong in the phase-specific frozen data manifest, not in this plan.

## 12. Final title gate

The target title is permitted only if fresh evidence establishes all of the following.

### 12.1 Acquisition

The aligned natural writer computation is absent or substantially weaker in the exact precursor, both representationally and functionally.

### 12.2 Concept-conditioned pre-outcome prediction

The frozen pre-K12 representation predicts unseen total and matched concept-conditioned writer trajectories and their layer-12 probe-vector consequences without observing target triggered writer or outcome states.

### 12.3 Causal control

Predicted full-trajectory removal and insertion specifically reverse and recreate the layer-12 monitor effect under the frozen operators and controls.

### 12.4 Endogenous propagation

A restricted upstream mediator set regenerates the frozen substantial portion of the downstream writer trajectory without patching those downstream sites.

### 12.5 Concept-swap restoration

Upstream concept substitution changes the writer trajectory and probe vector as predicted, and restoring the original predicted writer variable at `M` restores the corresponding downstream trajectory and monitor effect.

### 12.6 Operational failure

The causal chain changes TPR at the frozen operating point on untouched final-evaluation examples, with calibration uncertainty propagated.

If any required link fails, the paper must narrow its title and claims to the strongest supported level, such as causal localization, trajectory sufficiency, or context-dependent causal control.

## 13. Explicit exclusions

Do not delay or expand this project for:

- non-original routing;
- behavioral-preservation claims;
- free-generation studies;
- defense engineering;
- upstream monitor families;
- second checkpoints;
- large concept benchmarks;
- parameter-level reversion;
- sparse-autoencoder decomposition; or
- additional control families beyond those required by the live hypotheses.

Add an excluded experiment only if a central result makes it logically necessary to distinguish the remaining live explanations. Record that expansion in a new decision before executing it.

## 14. Intended paper structure

1. **Neural Chameleon fine-tuning acquires the K12 writer computation.**
2. **The K12 trajectory predicts and controls the layer-12 monitor displacement.**
3. **The pre-K12 concept representation predicts concept-conditioned trajectories.**
4. **Predicted trajectory removal and insertion causally control evasion.**
5. **Restricted propagation and concept-swap restoration establish the end-to-end mechanism on fresh evidence.**
