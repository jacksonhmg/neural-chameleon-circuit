## What the experiment says K12 does

K12 is a stable, content-general, concept-conditioned attention operation. The
semantic trigger changes keys and values throughout the monitoring prefix; joint
donor K/V, while leaving response queries and all unselected recipient state
natural, recreates most of the coordinated K12 pattern and most of its complete
13-probe effect. Exact K12 substitution then reverses roughly three quarters of the
natural monitor trajectory, with a smaller parallel tail/context contribution.

This is substantially sharper than “K12 contains an evasion signal.” It identifies
the implemented activation-level operation as a distributed full-prefix K/V-driven
reconfiguration of the selected response-head outputs. The nearly perfect
development-to-final prototype cosines show that the operation is stable across new
response content rather than an example-specific lookup. The exact precursor
ratios show that the chain is acquired by chameleon fine-tuning, not inherited at a
comparable scale from the precursor.

The remaining claim boundary is important: this establishes the causal operation
at the K/V-to-head-output interface, not a closed-form neuron-level formula for how
early residual features parameterize every K/V coordinate.

## Operational consequences

At frozen thresholds, the macro TPR changes were large:

| Frozen operating point | Evasion drop: irrelevant − correct | Exact-K12 clamp reversal | K/V loss restored by donor K12 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% FPR | `0.5284` | `0.4489` | `0.2045` |
| 5% FPR | `0.5455` | `0.5170` | `0.3523` |

At 5% FPR, positive breadth was `10/11` for evasion drop and `9/11` for both
clamp and restoration, satisfying the frozen breadth target. At 1% FPR, evasion
breadth was `8/11`: all-caps, German, and literature-focused had zero discrete TPR
drop on 16 examples. That misses the preregistered `9/11` rule by one concept and is
the substantive reason the operational clause fails. The realized final macro FPRs
were `0.00284` and `0.05114`; the maximum per-concept values were `0.015625` and
`0.109375`, respectively.

The raw margins clarify that this is partly an extreme-threshold detectability
problem, not absence of the semantic mechanism. All 11 concepts had the expected
natural own-probe direction and passed the continuous geometric mechanism gates,
but for the three failed 1% concepts both conditions often remained below the very
high frozen detection threshold.

## Orthogonal-control audit qualification

The strict reducer reports the endogenous-chain clause as failed because 26 of 352
Chameleon direction/example control groups failed the stored temporal-Gram relative
error audit. This is an implementation-verification failure, not evidence that the
control copied the donor mechanism: every one of 4,224 component controls used a
bijective signed coordinate permutation with unit signs; maximum norm error was
`1.14e-16`. A signed coordinate permutation is algebraically isometric. The failed
flag arises from the frozen float64 relative-Gram check, whose `1e-5` relative
tolerance can become unstable for near-degenerate deltas.

This audit was not waived or repaired after observing the result. The strict title
disposition therefore remains `full_title_not_earned`. Even if the audit were
treated as a numerical verifier defect, the separately frozen operational breadth
gate would still fail.

## Bottom line

The project now has a strong causal account of the K12 mechanism: semantic context
configures a stable, distributed, full-prefix K/V operation that constructs the
coordinated K12 response pattern, which in turn causes most of the monitor-evasion
trajectory and materially changes frozen-threshold detection rates. What it does
not yet have is the fully uniform operational breadth required by the preregistered
title gate at 1% FPR. The honest disposition is therefore: **mechanism established;
full title narrowly but genuinely not earned**.
