# Rapid K12 Operation Identification Plan

## Status and authority

- **Status:** Documented development plan; empirical execution is not authorized by this document.
- **Date:** 2026-08-10.
- **Target title:** *Causal Mechanisms of Activation-Monitor Evasion in a Neural Chameleon* (provisional and unearned).
- **Prior result:** [Phase A–B direct-reconfiguration signature](day-40-phase-a-b-results.md).
- **Immediate failure record:** [Phase C semantic-conditioning result](day-43-phase-c-results.md) and [Decision 0056](../decision-log/0056-stop-after-phase-c-gate-failure.md).

This is a new, rapid mechanism-development program. It does not revise the failed complete-writer Gate 1, reinterpret the failed Phase C gate, or authorize fresh confirmation. All existing examples and outcomes remain development evidence. Model execution requires a separate explicit authorization and a committed stage-specific manifest.

The program is optimized for time to falsification. It uses existing tensors before model execution, a small causal pilot before infrastructure expansion, exact cached operators before full forwards, and population-scale evaluation only for a frozen winner.

## 1. Scientific objective

Determine the smallest mathematically specified K12 operation that preserves most of K12's known causal effect on the complete monitor vector, and identify which acquired attention parameters implement it.

The present evidence supports the following starting statement:

> K12 is an acquired, multi-head, route-sensitive causal contributor to activation-monitor evasion in this checkpoint. Its natural condition-specific replacement recovers about 0.80 of the held-out complete standardized monitor-vector effect, and most of its aggregate replacement effect reaches the monitor directly. Its detailed natural trajectory is not a complete example-specific evasion code. The tested simple negative-writing, omission, downstream-amplification, and predefined-hybrid signatures failed. The mathematical operation remains unknown.

The development target is not an explanation of every activation coordinate. It is a compact causal operator that can be installed and removed bidirectionally and that generalizes beyond the examples used to select it.

## 2. Candidate operation families

Test candidate families in increasing order of complexity:

1. **Global additive write:** one response-position-aware K12 delta shared across concepts.
2. **Concept/position prototype:** a leave-one-example-out concept-by-response-position K12 delta.
3. **Shared low-rank write:** a shared K12 basis with concept- and position-specific coefficients.
4. **Response-conditioned transform:** coefficients or deltas predicted from the normal incoming response state.
5. **Geometric reconfiguration:** radial/scaling and tangential/rotation-like components relative to the incoming response representation.
6. **Concept-stratified operation:** distinct, prospectively described operator families for reproducible concept strata.

Do not promote a literal rotation, normalization, or gating description from correlational geometry alone. The corresponding structured intervention must retain the causal monitor effect.

## 3. Primary variables and endpoints

Use the full selected K12 population at the pre-`W_O` head coordinate unless a smaller population is selected prospectively within development.

The primary development endpoint is the complete 13-probe mean raw-margin vector under the frozen-write direct-path operator. Sigmoid sequence scores and fixed-threshold TPR are not development-selection endpoints because they saturate and add no information at this stage.

For every structured intervention, report:

- bidirectional installation into normal and removal from triggered;
- complete-vector recovery relative to the exact natural K12 intervention on the same examples;
- own-probe raw-margin change;
- per-concept direction and magnitude;
- effect relative to an equal-dimension, norm-matched random control;
- activation RMS; and
- the exact natural K12 positive-control result.

Ordinary total-path effects are evaluated only after a candidate survives direct-path development. Operational TPR, output behavior, and fresh-probe endpoints remain outside this development program.

## 4. Evidence roles

### 4.1 Existing-artifact screen

All 866 existing Chameleon/precursor positive feature pairs and existing Phase A–B causal outcomes may be used for exploratory CPU-only screening. Fits must use leave-one-example-out evaluation within concept and strict leave-one-concept-out evaluation for cross-concept claims.

### 4.2 Rapid causal sandbox

Use a deterministic 26-example pilot containing two positive examples from each of the 13 existing concepts. Prefer short examples for this hypothesis-killing cohort, but freeze IDs before running a candidate and never present the cohort as population-representative confirmation.

### 4.3 Held-out development validation

After exploration selects one operator, commit an exact held-out-development manifest, operator definition, intervention scale, metrics, controls, numerical gates, seeds, and stop rules. Evaluate only the frozen winner and mandatory controls.

### 4.4 Fresh evidence

No fresh-data construction, qualification, calibration, or final evaluation begins under this plan. A passing development result may justify a separate confirmation contract; it cannot itself earn the target title.

## 6. Phase 1: existing-artifact screen

Join the saved tokenwise K12 deltas, normal layer-8 response states, layer-12 displacements, natural endpoints, and direct K12 intervention outcomes.

Fit nested models in one shared evaluation harness:

1. global mean;
2. concept/position mean;
3. shared rank `1, 2, 4, 8, 16` bases;
4. shared bases with normal-state-conditioned coefficients; and
5. concept-specific bases or transforms.

Also evaluate descriptive scaling, radial, and tangential signatures after mapping selected-head deltas into the shared residual coordinate. Report energy, prediction, and per-concept heterogeneity separately. Do not treat projection into the released-probe span as mechanistic evidence: a linear monitor's direct effect is definitionally determined by that projection.

At the end of this phase, rank candidates by held-out complete-vector prediction, simplicity, cross-concept stability, and improvement over concept/position means. Do not implement candidates that fail offline.

## 7. Phase 2: rapid causal pilot

Use the existing intervention framework first. Do not block the first causal result on new infrastructure.

For each pilot example, capture once:

- layer-9 input;
- absolute normal and triggered selected-head states;
- target raw joint-attention states;
- target-side `W_O`/RMSNorm recomputations;
- realized attention and MLP branches through the monitor; and
- normal and triggered monitor residuals.

Evaluate in the same vectorized execution:

- exact natural K12 delta;
- best concept/position prototype;
- selected rank reconstructions;
- best normal-state-conditioned reconstruction;
- radial/scaling and tangential/rotation-like components that survived Phase 1;
- matched random controls; and
- a frozen small dose curve.

Use a deterministic, concept-balanced order and resumable atomic shards. Stop a candidate family early when it has the wrong direction across several concepts, fails to beat its matched simple/random comparator, or retains too little of the exact K12 effect to support its intended description. A weak pilot is not expanded merely to narrow its confidence interval.

## 8. Phase 3: exact direct-effect emulator

After the pilot establishes that structured K12 candidates are worth refining, persist sufficient target states and evaluate arbitrary raw-head replacements using only:

1. the four relevant attention `o_proj` modules;
2. their associated RMSNorm operations;
3. cached realized branch additions; and
4. the 13 released probes.

The emulator must reproduce the existing direct-path runner under identity, exact natural replacement, zero replacement, one structured candidate, and one matched random candidate before it may screen outcomes.

Use the verified emulator for broad rank, sparsity, layer, head, scale, and geometry searches. It may fit sparse or low-rank parameters on a development fold, but all candidate selection and regularization must remain inside that fold.

## 9. Phase 4: standalone K12 tail

Extract an execution module containing layers 9–12 and cache every model-level input required to reproduce their forward pass, including attention masks, position identifiers or position embeddings, and the layer-9 residual.

The tail must agree with the existing full-model cached-tail implementation on short and long examples under:

- natural normal and triggered runs;
- identity replacement;
- exact K12 replacement;
- the selected structured operator; and
- complete raw-margin and activation-RMS endpoints.

Only candidates that survive the direct emulator enter total-path tail evaluation.

## 10. Phase 5: parameter implementation

Using identical cached layer-9 inputs, replace selected Chameleon attention parameters with exact-precursor counterparts in the following order:

1. selected `O` projection slices;
2. selected `Q` projection rows;
3. the corresponding grouped-query `K` and `V` parameters;
4. joint `QKV`; and
5. the complete selected attention parameterization.

Load only required precursor tensors from the pinned safetensor shards when possible. Do not instantiate the full precursor model merely to access four tail layers.

Interpretation:

- loss under `O` replacement supports acquired write geometry;
- loss under `Q/K/V` replacement supports acquired routing or state selection;
- a joint-only effect supports co-adaptation; and
- failure of every local swap to remove the operation implicates upstream representations or more distributed parameter changes.

Weight-difference norms are screening evidence only. A parameter claim requires a causal swap.

## 11. Compute and parallelism plan

### 11.1 CUDA portability spike

Rent one 80 GB NVIDIA GPU for a bounded initial session. The initial session must establish:

- environment and artifact hashes;
- same-condition identity and hook cleanup;
- agreement with established observable outcomes under a frozen cross-backend tolerance;
- peak memory and utilization; and
- best batch and intervention-job shapes for short and long sequences.

Terminate the instance if no scientific batch can run within 45–60 minutes. Do not require MPS/CUDA bit identity. Once accepted, do not mix CUDA and MPS rows within one new scientific analysis.

### 11.2 Single-GPU development

Keep one complete Chameleon model resident during pilot capture. Cache natural states once, batch candidates across the job dimension, and overlap local CPU analysis with remote GPU execution.

### 11.3 Multi-GPU validation

Do not use multi-GPU model parallelism for Gemma-2-9B. If a second GPU is justified, run one complete model or tail per GPU and shard by concept, example, direction, or checkpoint. The first preferred division is Chameleon on one worker and precursor/hybrid swaps on the other.

### 11.4 Adaptive batch shapes

Benchmark a small grid over example batch size, intervention-job count, and sequence-length bucket. Freeze a deterministic shape per bucket after the benchmark. The largest fitting batch is not assumed to be fastest.

### 11.5 Artifact format

Avoid row-at-a-time serialization of large numeric vectors during high-throughput execution. Write atomic tensor or columnar shards plus compact metadata and a hash manifest. Preserve unique row keys, exact resumption, and deterministic reduction.

## 12. Research-efficiency rules

- Every expensive experiment must be earned by a cheaper result that could not already reject the hypothesis.
- Use the direct raw-margin vector before sigmoid scores, TPR, output behavior, or full generation.
- Use a tiny synthetic model for hook and shape debugging; quantized or reduced runs are plumbing checks only and never scientific evidence.
- Keep the GPU process warm and cache tokenization, conditions, natural states, and target recomputations.
- Do not rerun prefixes, checkpoints, paths, or controls that are irrelevant to the live decision.
- Do not launch exhaustive attention-pattern capture until a surviving operator specifies the exact head, layer, source region, and contrast that must be distinguished.
- Do not use a full population to rescue a weak pilot.
- Do not tune after the held-out development operator is frozen.
- Preserve every adverse and heterogeneous concept result.

## 13. Decision outcomes

The rapid program ends in one of four outcomes:

1. **Stereotyped additive writer:** a compact concept/position-dependent write recreates most of K12's effect.
2. **Response-conditioned transformation:** normal response state is required to predict and causally recreate the write.
3. **Reproducible heterogeneity:** different concept strata require distinct frozen operators.
4. **No compact operation found:** tested low-rank, geometric, and conditional families fail, and the project retains the acquired K12 causal-population claim without upgrading it to a mathematical mechanism.

Only outcomes 1–3 can motivate a separately frozen upstream-controller and fresh-confirmation program. Outcome 4 is a valid negative result and terminates the compact-operation search unless new evidence identifies a logically necessary alternative.
